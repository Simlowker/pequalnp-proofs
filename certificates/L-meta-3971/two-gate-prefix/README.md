# Two freely reusable gates in Petersen subproblem L-meta-3971

The primary inputs are the nine bits x₀,…,x₈, together with x₁ OR x₈. Both constants are available. Form one two-input AND or OR gate and one NOT gate from these inputs, in either order. Either gate may use the earlier result, or they may be independent. Give both results and all primary inputs to an AND/OR tree. For every choice, the tree needs **at least 13 further gates** to compute the target. The two-gate prefix and the tree together need at least 15.

Both prefix outputs may be reused arbitrarily. The conclusion therefore excludes a 14-gate circuit whose reusable binary gate and sole inverter can be computed from primary inputs in just two gates, with all remaining live binary gates used at most once. It leaves open circuits with other gate sharing, or where a more complicated subexpression feeds the reused gate or the inverter.

Run `python3 verify_two_gate3971.py` after extracting the archive. The verifier uses Python's standard library and exact integer arithmetic. It checks the graph labels and independently constructs all 1,190 named configurations of the two signals. A binary gate on two distinct primary signals gives 45 input pairs and two operations. For each of the 90 choices, the inverter may complement any of the ten primary signals or the binary result. A further 200 configurations combine a complemented primary with any primary through either binary operation. Repeated arguments, empty or unary gates, and constant arguments yield constants, primary signals, complements, or an already available result; these smaller bases are contained in the enumerated cases. In particular, complementing a constant can be represented by ignoring the corresponding extra leaf.

For each configuration, a signed matrix is indexed by positive and negative assignments. At an outer OR choose a true child on each positive row. At an AND choose a false child on each negative column. This partitions the full matrix into disjoint leaf rectangles. Every such rectangle lies inside the rows where its leaf is true and columns where it is false. The verifier checks that any subrectangle in this separator has weight at most D for each possible leaf, including both reusable signals and both constants. The full matrix has weight greater than 13D, ruling out a tree with at most 13 leaves. A fan-in-two tree with at most 12 binary gates has at most 13 leaves. The checker includes empty subrectangles and accounts for negative entries by maximizing over all column selections for each selected row set.

This is a finite certificate checked by the supplied Python program. The full circuit lower bound remains open.
